# 10 — Story and Region Design

> How official games, the best hacks, and the best fangames tell (or fail to tell) a story — and a method for writing ours.

## The official story, reduced to a skeleton

Almost every mainline game is this:

1. A child leaves home with a gift animal
2. A rival mirrors them
3. Eight trials of increasing pageantry
4. A villain team with a philosophy that is either cartoon (Rocket) or almost-interesting (Plasma, Galactic)
5. A legendary Pokémon as a weather event
6. The child is the only competent person in the region
7. Champion, credits, legendary laundry list

This skeleton *works* for 10-year-olds and for comfort replays. It is also why adults migrate to Reborn, Insurgence, Colosseum, and Legends Arceus.

## What actually worked in official fiction

| Game | Why the story is remembered |
|---|---|
| **Gen 5 BW** | N, ideology, "are Pokémon friends or tools." The series' best argument |
| **Colosseum / XD** | You are an adult. Shadow Pokémon. Orre is ugly. Tone match |
| **Platinum** | Cyrus's nihilism is a real idea, even if the ending is beams |
| **Legends Arceus** | You are a stranger in a pre-League world. Research as plot |
| **Sun/Moon** | Found family, Lillie. Uneven, but character-forward |
| **HGSS** | Not deep, but *place* and music do the writing |
| **Z-A pitch** | City as character, redevelopment as conflict. Good instinct |

## What fan fiction added

| Work | Story move |
|---|---|
| **Reborn** | The city is the villain and the home. Crime, pollution, incompetent institutions |
| **Rejuvenation** | Catastrophe history, long character arcs, episode TV structure |
| **Insurgence** | Cults, deltas, "religion but make it Pokémon" |
| **Unbound** | Competent, slightly mature, not grimdark. Optional dark route |
| **Rocket Edition** | Moral camera flip. You *are* the grunt |
| **Uranium** | Nuclear history as regional original sin |
| **Realidea System** | Artificial bonds — thematically closest to us |
| **Snakewood** | Horror, plague type. Commitment to nastiness |

## Tropes to retire or invert

1. **The child savior.** Play as a junior warden, a disgraced researcher, a migrant worker, or a 20-year-old dropout. Age is tone.
2. **The League is purely good.** Make it a utility company / colonial science bureau. Useful, compromised.
3. **Villain team of color-coded teens.** One ideology, internal dissent, people who leave.
4. **Legendary as final beam fight.** Legendary as *ecology* or *legal person*.
5. **Silent protagonist.** At least give dialogue stances (BW did this a little). ECHOES should have a voiced-in-text personality with player lean.
6. **Rival who exists to lose.** A rival who beats you and *keeps* a win on the record.
7. **Gyms as typed rooms.** Keep trials. Change what they *mean* (license, pilgrimage, inspection).
8. **Post-game legend laundry.** Post-game should be a second argument, not a checklist.

## Region design method

Do not draw a map and staple a plot. Do this order:

### 1. Thesis sentence

ECHOES: **"A world that can still be heard is being tuned into a product, and you are a gifted tuner."**

### 2. Geographic metaphor

Pick a real-world collage, then distort hard.

Good official examples: Kalos = France-as-pageant. Unova = NYC + rust belt. Alola = Hawai'i + tourism critique (half-finished). Hisui = Edo Hokkaido.

For ECHOES, a strong collage:

- A drowned river delta + a rusting radio city + highland monasteries that still keep silence
- Think: New Orleans + Pripyat exclusion-zone edges + Kyoto hills, none of them literal

Name working: **the Vesper Reach** (or **Isonde**, **Marrowcoast**, **the Relay**).

### 3. Three cultures, not eight towns of the same NPC

| Culture | Relationship to Echoes | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| **Relay cities** | Industrial attunement, League HQ, licenses | Neon, trams, brass, rain |
| **Tide communes** | Coexistence, old pacts, suspicious of balls-as-cages | Wood, salt, bells |
| **Highland keepers** | Silence as sacred, some Echoes must not be bound | Stone, fog, prayer flags |

Towns are mixtures. Conflict is zoning, not "evil team HQ in a cave."

### 4. Institutions instead of Teams

- **The Resonance League** — regulates pacts, sells licenses, runs the circuit
- **Chorus Mercantile** — private harvesters, our "Rocket" but with lobbyists
- **The Unbound Ear** — radicals who free Echoes, sometimes violently
- **Keepers** — monks / scientists who maintain Field shrines

The player should be able to agree with *someone* in each.

### 5. Character list before map

Minimum viable cast:

- **Player** — a newly licensed Attuner (age ~19). Not silent. Three stance sliders: Mercy / Ambition / Inquiry
- **Anchor** (professor analogue) — your mentor, already compromised by League funding
- **Rook** (rival) — better than you, believes in the League sincerely
- **Nyx** (deuteragonist) — a Keeper exile who hears Silence coming
- **Warden Sol** — League inspector, sometimes ally, sometimes lock
- **Maestro Venn** — Chorus executive, charming, not a cartoon
- **Eight Circuit Inspectors** — not "Gym Leaders of Type." Each is a *test of practice* (ecology, ethics, combat, craft, history…)
- **The Hollow Choir** — late-game legendary cluster: Echoes that used to be a city

### 6. Plot spine (3 acts)

**Act I — License**  
Earn the right to carry more than one pact. Discover a Silence bloom on Route 3 that official maps mark as "cleared."

**Act II — Audit**  
The League sends you to inspect Chorus sites. You find legal harvest that is killing Fields. Rook refuses to see it. Nyx asks you to sabotage a relay. Player choice forks reputation, not the existence of Act III.

**Act III — Retune or Mute**  
A capital Field collapses. The Hollow Choir wakes. Endings:

- **Reform** — take the Champion-analogue seat, bind the Choir, change the law (bittersweet, system persists)
- **Release** — break the relay network, Echoes go feral-free, cities darken, freedom with a cost
- **Chorus** — side with Mercantile, "save" people by caging the wild (evil-adjacent, fully written, not a joke)
- **Keeper** — walk away from power, maintain one living Field. Small ending, spiritually the hidden best

Unbound's dark/normal toggle is a light version of this. We should make the fork *mechanical* (who still talks to you, which Echoes will pact with you).

## Writing craft rules

- NPCs have jobs that are not "Pokémon trainer"
- Every town has one problem you can ignore and one you cannot
- Ban speeches longer than a screen without a choice or a joke
- Swear or don't. If we swear, it is rare
- Do not write "edgy" as a substitute for "specific"
- Humor is mandatory even in a sad world (Unbound and Cassette Beasts know this)
- Translate-friendly: no untranslatable puns in *plot-critical* lines
- A style guide: second person vs first, how Echoes are pronounced, what the League calls a pact

## Music and tone

Unbound's original OST is half of why it feels like a real game. Cassette Beasts' soundtrack is a character.

Budget reality:

- 1.0 needs ~20 strong loops: title, town A/B, wild day/night, cave, city rain, battle wild, battle trainer, battle inspector, battle rival, legendary, ending
- Commission a small pack or use original stems
- Do not rip GBA soundfonts of official tracks

## What not to write

- "The chosen one of the prophecy" unless it is a lie the League printed
- Torture as character development
- A dead mom as the only female motivation
- Real-world sacred texts pasted onto types
- A 120-hour visual novel we cannot finish

Target runtime for 1.0 story: **18–25 hours** main, 35 with side pacts. Rejuvenation can be 100 hours because it already exists.
